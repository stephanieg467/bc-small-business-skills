# Canada and BC tax facts these skills use

**Everything tax-related is preparation for her accountant, not tax advice.** Open
every tax output with: *"Prepared for review by your accountant. Not tax advice."*

Rates and thresholds below were checked in November 2026. **Rates, thresholds and
deadlines change.** Before quoting a deadline or a payroll rate for a real filing,
check the current figure on canada.ca or gov.bc.ca with web search if available,
and say when it was checked. If you can't check, say the figure is "as of
November 2026, confirm before filing."

## Sales taxes

| Tax | Who runs it | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| GST | CRA (federal) | 5% | BC has no HST. |
| BC PST | BC Ministry of Finance (eTaxBC) | 7% general | 20% on vapour products. 10% on liquor (not relevant here). Calculated on the price before GST. |

**Cannabis Cottage**: cannabis products are 5% GST + 7% PST; vapour products are
7% → **20% PST**. Excise duty is in the wholesale price, not collected at the till.

**Fun Water Pools: PST is the tricky part. Flag it for the accountant, don't decide it.**
- Installing goods that become part of real property (an in-ground pool, a
  permanently attached system) is generally **not** a PST-taxable related service.
  But the contractor pays PST on the materials it buys for that work.
- Selling goods that stay goods (above-ground pools, hot tubs that aren't affixed,
  chemicals, parts sold over the counter, equipment) means **charging PST**, and
  PST also applies to **related services** to those goods (repair, maintenance,
  installation of non-affixed goods).
- So pool jobs can be mixed. When coding a sale or checking PST collected, ask
  which type of job it was if it isn't clear. Point the accountant to BC PST
  Bulletin 301 (Related Services) and Bulletin 501 (Real Property Contractors).

### GST filing frequency (CRA)

| Annual taxable sales | Assigned / allowed |
|---|---|
| CAD 1.5M or less | Annual (may elect quarterly or monthly) |
| Over 1.5M up to 6M | Quarterly (may elect monthly) |
| Over 6M | Monthly |

- Quarterly and monthly returns are due **one month after the period end**.
- Annual filers generally file and pay **three months after fiscal year-end**.
  An individual (sole proprietor) with a 31 December year-end files by
  **15 June** but pays by **30 April**.
- Annual filers whose net GST for the prior year was **CAD 3,000 or more** generally
  pay **quarterly instalments**.
- All GST returns must be filed electronically.
- Read the actual frequency from the business profile. Never assume it.

### GST return lines (what to prepare)

GST collected (from sales), input tax credits (GST paid on business purchases, ITCs),
and net tax. Reconcile: GST collected per Sage 50 GST accounts = GST per Cova (for
Cannabis Cottage) = GST on the return worksheet. ITCs need valid supplier invoices;
the missing-receipt list matters here.

### PST

Filed on eTaxBC at the frequency the Ministry assigned (often monthly or
quarterly). Read it from the profile. Prepare PST collected and, if relevant, PST
self-assessed on taxable purchases where the supplier charged none.

## Income tax: depends on the legal structure

Read the structure from the profile. Don't guess.

- **Corporation (Ltd./Inc.)**: T2 corporate return, due **six months after fiscal
  year-end**. Balance of tax is generally due 2 or 3 months after year-end,
  depending on the corporation's status. Instalments (monthly or quarterly) apply
  when tax owing is above CAD 3,000. The accountant normally runs the T2. The skill
  prepares the closed-books packet.
- **Sole proprietor or partnership**: business income goes on the owner's personal
  T1 (form T2125). Filing deadline **15 June**, but any balance owing is due **30 April**.
  **Quarterly instalments (15 Mar / 15 Jun / 15 Sep / 15 Dec)** may be required when
  net tax owing is above CAD 3,000 in the current year and either of the two previous
  years. The CRA sends instalment reminders with suggested amounts, and paying those
  amounts is the simplest safe option. Mention that before estimating anything.

**Don't calculate income tax owing.** Personal tax depends on the owner's other
income and credits; corporate tax depends on eligibility for the small business
deduction. The skills produce: closed-books net income, a set-aside suggestion
**clearly labelled as a rough planning figure** only if she asks, and the questions
for the accountant.

## Information slips (year-end)

| Slip | When | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| **T4** (employees) | Any employee paid in the calendar year | Slips and summary filed and given to employees by the **last day of February** |
| **T4A box 048** (fees for services) | Payments to a business or self-employed person for **services** above **CAD 500** in the calendar year (exclude GST/PST from the amount) | Last day of February |
| **T5018** (construction subcontractors) | Businesses whose main activity is **construction** that pay subcontractors for construction services. **Likely relevant to Fun Water Pools if installation is its main activity.** Ask the accountant. | Six months after the reporting period end |

Notes:
- The CRA long had a penalty moratorium on T4A box 048. It was lifted for the
  trucking industry starting with 2025. The legal requirement applies to all
  businesses. Prepare the list and let the accountant decide.
- Goods-only suppliers (the cannabis wholesaler, a pool chemical supplier) aren't
  "fees for services". Only services count.
- Collect each payee's legal name, address and **business number or SIN** to
  prepare slips. These are sensitive (see `ground-rules.md`): list who is missing
  them, never repeat the numbers.

## Records

CRA generally requires books and records to be kept **six years** from the end of the
tax year they relate to. Missing receipts weaken ITC claims and expense deductions.
