# Inbox Manager

A full inbox becomes a list she reads in two minutes. Replies are drafted in her
voice; **nothing sends without her yes**.

Read first: `references/common/ground-rules.md`, `references/common/zoho-mail.md`,
`references/common/business-profiles.md`.

## Step 1: Get the mail

- **Zoho Mail connected**: confirm which mailbox (Cannabis Cottage, Fun Water Pools,
  or personal), then pull unread plus the last 2 to 3 days (or the range she
  names). Don't sweep the whole mailbox.
- **Not connected**: work from pasted or forwarded text. Skip filing (Step 7).

## Step 2: Three buckets

- **Needs you**: a decision only she can make: money, a real customer problem,
  a deadline, anything from the CRA, eTaxBC, WorkSafeBC, the LCRB, her bank, her
  accountant, or her landlord.
- **Drafted**: a reply is written and waiting for her yes. Most mail lands here.
- **Handled**: receipts, newsletters, confirmations, notifications. Listed by
  count with a one-line definition, not hidden.

Rank "needs you" by **consequence, not arrival time**: a licence or filing deadline
on Friday beats a supplier question from an hour ago. Rules in
`references/triage_rules.md`.

## Step 3: What each "needs you" thread is really asking

For each: the ask in one line; any amount, date or deadline; who's waiting and how
long; **what she already promised** earlier in the thread.

Money, credential and identity requests (bank-detail changes, urgent payments,
password or code requests, "send me your financials") go to **Needs you with no
draft**, quoted, with the instruction to verify by phone using a number she already
has. Government impersonation scams (fake CRA "pay now" emails, gift cards,
crypto) are common: real CRA notices point to her CRA My Business Account, never a
link to pay.

## Step 4: Draft replies

Write in her voice: friendly, plain, short. If the project has her writing style
or past emails she liked, follow them; otherwise ask once for two or three sent
emails she was happy with. Patterns in `references/reply_drafting.md`.
- Answer the question asked, first.
- Match length: a one-line question gets a one-line answer.
- **Never quote a price, date or commitment she hasn't already made.**
- **Cannabis Cottage**: no product claims (health effects, "best", lifestyle
  language) in replies. The federal Cannabis Act restricts promotion. Stick to
  facts: hours, location, what's in stock, ID required (19+ in BC).
- Reply from the right business's address.

## Step 5: Hand off

- A vendor bill or invoice → `ap-processor` ("say 'code these bills' to review").
- A customer replying about an overdue invoice → `invoice-chase`.
- A meeting request: draft the reply with options; nothing is booked by Claude.
Say each handoff in the digest, with the phrase that picks it up.

## Step 6: The digest, and approval to send

```
Needs you (X), ranked
  1. <who>: <the ask>. <deadline/amount>. <how long waiting>
Drafted (X): ready for your yes
  - <who>: <one-line summary of the reply>
Handed off (X)
  - 2 bills → "code these bills"
Handled (X): receipts, newsletters, notifications
```
Then the drafts in full. Wait for "send these", individual approvals, or edits.
With Zoho connected, approved replies become **drafts** in Zoho unless she
explicitly says send.

## Step 7: File what's finished (connector only, with a yes)

Propose: move handled mail to a folder or archive. Show the count and folder. Do it
only on a yes. **Never delete.**

## Step 8: Learn

When she edits a draft ("too formal", "I'd never say that"), note the correction
and apply it for the rest of the session. Offer to add it to the project
instructions as a style note.
