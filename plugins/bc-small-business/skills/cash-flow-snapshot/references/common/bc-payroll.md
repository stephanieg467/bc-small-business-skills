# BC payroll rules these skills check against

**Prepared for review, not legal or payroll advice.** Rules below were checked in
November 2026 against gov.bc.ca and canada.ca. Rates change, often on 1 January
(CPP, EI, tax tables) and **1 June (BC minimum wage)**. Before relying on a rate,
check it with web search if available, or have her use the **CRA Payroll Deductions
Online Calculator (PDOC)** or the Sage 50 payroll tax tables (if her Sage payroll
plan is current).

## Who calculates deductions

Claude doesn't compute CPP, CPP2, EI or income tax withholding to the cent. Those come
from:
- the **Sage 50 payroll module** (needs a current Sage payroll subscription for
  up-to-date tax tables), or
- the **CRA PDOC** (free, online), or
- her payroll service.

Claude's job is **hours, gross pay, and anomaly checks** before the run, plus a
sanity check that deductions look plausible (e.g. a CPP deduction of zero for an
employee under the annual maximum is a flag).

## BC Employment Standards: the checks

| Rule | What to check |
|---|---|
| **Minimum wage** | General minimum wage CAD 18.25/hour from 1 June 2026 (adjusted every 1 June). Flag any hourly rate below the current figure. |
| **Daily overtime** | Over 8 hours in a day: 1.5x. Over 12 hours in a day: 2x. BC counts **daily** overtime, not just weekly. This is the most commonly missed rule. |
| **Weekly overtime** | Over 40 hours in a week: 1.5x, counting only the first 8 hours worked each day (so daily overtime isn't paid twice). |
| **Averaging agreements / overtime banking** | Different rules apply only with a written agreement or the employee's written request. Ask if one exists before applying them. |
| **Minimum daily pay** | An employee who reports for work gets at least 2 hours' pay (4 hours if scheduled for more than 8). |
| **Statutory holidays** | 11 in BC: New Year's Day, Family Day, Good Friday, Victoria Day, Canada Day, BC Day, Labour Day, National Day for Truth and Reconciliation, Thanksgiving, Remembrance Day, Christmas Day. Easter Monday and Boxing Day are not BC stats. Eligible employees get an average day's pay; working the stat means time-and-a-half (double after 12 hours) plus the average day's pay. Flag any pay period containing a stat. |
| **Vacation pay** | At least 4% of gross wages once employed 5 calendar days (2 weeks' vacation after 1 year); 6% after 5 years (3 weeks). Check which method she uses (paid each cheque or accrued). |
| **Paid sick days** | 5 paid sick days a year after 90 days of employment. |
| **Pay timing** | Paid at least twice a month; all wages (including overtime and stat pay) paid within **8 days** after the pay period ends. |
| **Pay stub** | Every payday: hours, rates, overtime hours and rate, gross, deductions, net. |
| **Final pay** | Within 48 hours if the employer ends employment; within 6 days if the employee quits. Also triggers an **ROE (Record of Employment)**. |

## Remittances and year-end

- **Source deductions** (CPP, EI, income tax withheld, plus the employer's CPP and
  EI share) go to the CRA. Most small employers are "regular remitters": due by
  the **15th of the month after** the pay date. Her remitter type is on CRA
  correspondence, so put it in the profile.
- **WorkSafeBC**: premiums on assessable payroll, reported and paid on the
  schedule in her WorkSafeBC account (quarterly or annual). Year-end payroll
  reconciliation with WorkSafeBC is also required.
- **T4 slips and summary**: by the **last day of February**.
- **ROE**: issued when an employee has an interruption of earnings (quit, layoff,
  leave). Usually within 5 days for electronic ROEs.

## Two businesses, two payrolls

Each business has its own CRA payroll account (RP), its own WorkSafeBC account and
its own employees. Never combine them, even if someone works for both.
